Chem Illustrator privacy policy

Last updated 25 October 2026.

Chem Illustrator is a free, open-source program for drawing chemistry, made by Nathan O'Hare. It has no accounts, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking. The developer does not collect, receive or store any of your data.

Your files

Drawings, documents, slides and sheets stay on your device, or wherever you choose to save them (for example iCloud Drive through the Files app). Automatic backups and settings are kept on your device only.

When the app goes online

The app connects to the internet only when you use a feature that needs it, and only sends what that feature needs:

  • Look up on PubChem and compound properties: the structure or name you look up is sent to PubChem (US National Library of Medicine).
  • References: a DOI or search you enter is sent to doi.org or Crossref to fetch the citation.
  • AI Model (off unless you set it up): if you link an AI provider (such as Anthropic, OpenAI or Google) with your own key, the pictures or text you ask it to read are sent to that provider under your own account and its privacy policy. Your key is stored only on your device.
  • Structure recognition: the offline reader is downloaded once from GitHub. After that, pictures are read on your device.
  • Updates and feedback: the desktop app checks GitHub for new versions. Help > Send Feedback opens a GitHub page in your browser, where you choose what to send.

TestFlight

If you test the iPad app through TestFlight, Apple shares crash reports and any feedback you choose to send with the developer, under Apple's privacy policy.

Children

The app does not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children.

Contact

Questions: open an issue at github.com/nathanohare/Chem-Illustrator.
